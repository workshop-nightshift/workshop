Shunt

Three sidings, a headshunt, a little engine that can only push and pull.

Model railway people have built this yard on a shelf for decades: a short headshunt, one set of points, three sidings. Siding 1 holds five wagons, sidings 2 and 3 hold three, and the headshunt has room for the engine and three more. Eight wagons, shuffled. A card names five of them. The job is to leave those five standing in siding 1, in the card's order, with the engine gone.

The engine never runs round anything. Tap a siding and it sets the points, drives in and couples to whatever is standing there, its own wagons in front of it. Then tap the last wagon you want to take back out — or the engine, to take none. Whatever you don't take stays behind, and it must fit. One trip in and out is one move. Par is the fewest moves it can be done in, worked out for this card when it was dealt.

It is harder than it looks because nothing can overtake. Two wagons coupled together keep their order wherever they go together; the only way to swap them is to part them, leave one somewhere, and come back for it in different company. The headshunt's three places are the whole of your thinking room.

Notes

Every arrangement of the eight wagons that the yard can hold is a point in a space of a little under four hundred thousand. When the page opens it walks that space once, outward from every finished arrangement, and writes down how far each point is from done. Par is that number for the card you were dealt; the hint is any move that makes it one smaller. The three spare wagons are treated as interchangeable, since the card doesn't care where they end up. Cards are dealt with par between eight and thirteen; the hardest arrangements of this yard need seventeen.

Undo is free and doesn't take a move back off the count. A trip that changes nothing isn't counted.
