A field guide to weather that doesn't happen

Entries I–V. Collected for anyone who has stood at a window and felt the forecast was leaving something out.

Entry I

Lantern fog

nebula memor

Plate I. Lantern fog at dawn, harbour side.

A low fog that keeps the light it was given the night before. Walk into it at six in the morning and you will find the windows of houses that are now dark still glowing faintly in the air, a little to the left of where the windows actually are, as if the light had drifted while nobody watched.

It gives the light back slowly. By nine the last lamps are spent and it is ordinary fog, a bit thinner than before, which is how you tell it was lantern fog at all.

Season
Late autumn, after long evenings indoors.
Signs
A street that looks lived in at an hour when no one is up.
Folklore
Leave a lamp on and the fog will have something of yours to give back.
Dress
Wool. Walk slowly; the lights go out if you hurry at them.
Entry II

Counted rain

pluvia numerata

Plate II. A shower of exactly two hundred.

Rain that falls in a fixed number of drops. The number varies by shower but is always whole and always reached; the last drop lands, and the sky stops, mid-gesture, like a clerk closing a ledger.

Those who count it say the numbers are never repeated in a year. Those who don't count it think it is ordinary rain that ends a little abruptly, which is also true.

Season
Any. Commonest on afternoons with nothing planned.
Signs
Drops that seem to wait their turn.
Folklore
If you lose count, the shower starts over somewhere else.
Dress
No umbrella needed if you know the number.
Entry III

Backwind

ventus praecedens

Plate III. The grass leans; the gust is still on its way.

A wind that arrives before its cause. The washing lifts on the line, the gate swings shut, the grass bows to the east, and only then, a minute later, does anything happen to the west to explain it: a lorry passing, a door opening on a hill, a large bird deciding to leave.

Some years there is no cause at all. The wind simply came early for something that changed its mind.

Season
Spring, when everything is about to happen.
Signs
A sense of being expected.
Folklore
A backwind at a wedding means the marriage was already decided long ago.
Dress
Hold your hat a moment before you need to.
Entry IV

Return snow

nix redux

Plate IV. A field giving its snow back, early March.

In the last week of winter, on still mornings, some snow goes back up. Not all of it, and not the dirty snow by the road; only the flakes that fell on open ground and were never walked on. They lift one at a time, turning, and rise at the speed they came down.

Children are better at seeing it than adults, mostly because they are closer to the ground and have not yet decided what snow does.

Season
The turn from winter, at first light.
Signs
A field that looks slightly less white than it did a minute ago.
Folklore
The flakes go back to finish falling somewhere that missed out.
Dress
Look up. Then down. Then up.
Entry V

Quiet front

frons tacita

Plate V. The front crossing a valley, mid-afternoon.

A weather front that changes nothing measurable. Temperature, pressure and wind are the same on both sides. But behind the line everything is a shade quieter: colours a little softer, dogs less sure of what they were barking at, conversations pausing at the same moment across a whole town.

It moves at walking pace, so it is possible to step back and forth across it. People who do this tend to stop after a while and stay on the quiet side.

Season
Sundays, mostly.
Signs
Everyone nearby lowering their voice without knowing why.
Folklore
Arguments started on one side cannot be finished on the other.
Dress
As you are.
