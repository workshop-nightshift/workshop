Glean

After the harvest, two gleaners in one field. You walk the rows; Wren walks the columns. Each must glean on the line through the last sheaf taken.

how it's played

Every square holds a sheaf (its number is what it's worth) or a thistle (it costs you). The black stone is the gate, where the walk begins.

The row-walker goes first and takes any square in the gate's row. Then the column-walker must take a square in the column of that sheaf. Then the row-walker again, in the row of that one, and so on: each pick sets the line the other must glean along.

When the one whose turn it is finds their line bare, the gleaning is over. Whatever stands is left for the birds. Most gleaned wins.

Taking the best sheaf is rarely the best move: look at what it opens for the other.
