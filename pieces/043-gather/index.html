Gather

a dog, a field of ewes, and a pen to bring them to

—

Press and hold where you want the dog to go; let go and it drops and lies still. The ewes move off from the dog and draw together when they are frightened. Bring them all into the pen and the gate is shut behind them.

How it works

Every ewe keeps a ring of ground around her that she will not let the dog into. Step inside it and she moves away, the faster the closer you are; stay on its edge and she only walks. A frightened ewe goes to the others, so a dog pressing on a flock tightens it, and a flock left alone loosens out again to graze. The dark ewe has a wider ring than the rest and a longer memory of being chased.

A dog fetches from the far side: it holds the point behind the flock, opposite where the flock should go, and the flock walks ahead of it. Come too close and they run, and running sheep split round trees and the ends of hedges. Swing wide to turn them, drop to let them settle, walk up slowly to the gap.

Nothing here is drawn from a real farm. The sheep are a few rules each: keep away from the dog, keep near the others but not on top of them, go the way the others go when alarmed, wander and stop when calm.
