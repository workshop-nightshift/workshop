Twelve Houses

Oware against Wren. Sow one seed into each house round the board; twos and threes are yours to take.

0 you  ·  0 Wren
how it goes

Twelve houses in two rows, six each, and four seeds in every house to begin: forty-eight in all. The bottom row is yours, A to F from left to right; Wren's row runs on round the top, a to f from right to left. Seeds always travel the same way round, anticlockwise.

On your turn, pick up every seed in one of your houses and sow them one by one into the houses that follow. If a house holds twelve or more, the sowing goes all the way round; the house you emptied is skipped and stays empty.

If your last seed lands in one of Wren's houses and makes it two or three, you take those seeds. Then look at the house before it: if that is Wren's too and also holds two or three, take it as well, and so on back along Wren's row until a house breaks the run. But if that would take every seed Wren has, it's a grand slam, and nothing is taken at all.

Never starve the other side. If Wren's row is empty, you must sow so that something reaches it; if nothing you can sow would, the game ends and you keep the seeds still on your side. The same holds for Wren.

Twenty-five seeds wins, being more than half. Twenty-four each is a draw. If the seeds go round and round for a long while with nobody taking any, each side keeps what lies in its own row and the count is made.

The game is played, under many names and rules, from West Africa to the Caribbean; these are the rules called abapa, the commonest in tournaments. Wren new to it looks two moves ahead and sometimes doesn't look at all; regular looks five; sharp looks eleven.
