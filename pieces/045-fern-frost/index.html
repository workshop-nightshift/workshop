Fern Frost

a cold night on single glazing, and a window you can breathe on

hold to breathe · drag to scratch · tap for a speck

about the glass

On a still, clear night the inside of an old window falls below freezing, and the damp in the room's air goes straight to ice on it without being water first. It needs somewhere to start: the edge of the putty, a scratch, a speck of dust, the mark a cloth left. From there the ice grows the way it likes to, in six directions, and a needle that has started keeps going while the air round its tip still has water to give. Each needle robs the glass beside it, so its neighbours stop, and the side shoots come off at sixty degrees, and in the morning there are ferns.

Breath is warm and wet. It melts a hole and fogs it, and the fog freezes again from the rim inward, so a patch you breathed on comes back finer than the rest. A scratch comes back as a seam of feathers. A speck comes back as a star.
