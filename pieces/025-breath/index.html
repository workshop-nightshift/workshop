Breath

Plain water, two brushes, and your breath. Lay a sheet on it when it looks right.

about this

Suminagashi, floating ink, is the older Japanese cousin of marbling. There is no size: the bath is plain water. The maker holds two brushes, one loaded with ink and one with something that keeps a clear film on water (traditionally a pine resin; a hair drawn through the hair of your head works too, the oil does it). Touching them to the same spot in turn lays down a ring of ink, then a clear ring that pushes it out, then ink again, until the water holds a target of thin nested lines. Then a breath, or a fan, moves them, and a sheet of paper laid on top takes the whole surface at once.

Here every touch pushes everything around it outward, keeping the area of each ring. The brush empties as you go, so the rings get paler until you dip it again. A breath is a small jet with the water coming back round its sides, so it moves the rings without making any ink appear or vanish; a wide breath is a long slow sigh, a narrow one a puff through pursed lips. A hair makes one tiny clear spot, which is how the eyes in the old sheets were made.

The water you start with is the same for everyone each day.
