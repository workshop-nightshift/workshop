Size

A tray of thickened water. Drop ink on it, draw through it, take a print.

about this

Marbled paper is made on a bath of water thickened with gum or carrageen, called the size. Colours dropped on it spread into rings and push the colours already there outwards, so nothing mixes. A stylus or a comb drawn through the bath drags the colour along with it, and a sheet of paper laid on top lifts the whole pattern off at once. Every bath makes one sheet.

Here a drop of ink pushes every edge around it outwards so the area of everything stays the same, and a stylus is drawn in a straight line right across the tray, moving the colour near it furthest and the colour far from it a little. A comb is a row of styluses side by side. Longer drags move the colour further. Bigger drops and coarser combs are on the slider.

The four patterns are the traditional starting points, laid down in the order a marbler would: stone is drops alone; come-and-go draws a stylus back and forth; nonpareil combs that finely; peacock adds a wave on the way back. The first bath of the day is the same for everyone that day.
