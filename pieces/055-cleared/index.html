A pantoum: every line comes back once, and the last line is the first.

Tap a line to see where it comes back.

Write four lines. After that each stanza only asks for two; the other two are carried down, and the last stanza writes itself.

The pantoum came into French and English verse from the Malay pantun. Its rule is a ratchet: the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third of the next, so every line is said twice and the poem moves forward half a step at a time. To close, the two lines the first stanza left unrepeated, its first and third, come back in the last, and the poem ends on the line it began with.

The stitches on the left join each line to its return. Most are short, a stanza apart; the two long ones, in the warmer colour, are the lines that wait the whole poem.
