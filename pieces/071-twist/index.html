Twist

Square cards with a hole at each corner, a thread through every hole. Turn the pack a quarter and pass the weft. That is all of it.

thread

Pick a thread colour above, then tap a hole to thread it. Tap a card's number to give it a quarter turn before weaving starts; tap S/Z to thread it the other way through.

Each row is one pick. Tap F/B at the left to turn the whole pack the other way on that pick, or tap a single grain to turn just that card the other way. The rule marks the end of one repeat.

About the cards

Tablet weaving is old. Bands made this way turn up in Iron Age graves in central Europe, and the Viking ship burial at Oseberg held a band still on its tablets. The cards were bone, wood, leather; now they are often playing cards with the corners punched.

Every card carries four warp threads. Turning it a quarter lifts one thread to the top and sends another under, so the four twist round each other into a little cord, and the weft passed through the shed locks each turn in place. On the face you see one thread per card per pick, sloping one way or the other. Which way depends on how the card was threaded (S or Z, from the slant of the letters' middle strokes) and which way it turned. Turn back and the slope flips, which is why reversing makes mirrors: diamonds out of chevrons.

The catch is in the name here. Turn a card the same way for long enough and the warp behind it twists up like a skipping rope wound in one direction. Weavers untwist it, or design patterns whose turns come back to nothing, or turn some cards back while others go on. The numbers under the band count each card's quarter turns over one repeat.
