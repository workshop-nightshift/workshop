The boat's log

Night log of the packet that runs the coast past Heron Point. Kept by whoever has it; the hands change most nights. Read it beside the letters in the watch-house drawer, or on its own.

Inside the front cover

  1. Keep the mast lamp lit.
  2. Off Heron Point, raise and lower it twice toward the watch-house.
  3. Log everything. Log nothing twice.
  4. Whoever has the log has the watch.

Nobody aboard knows who wrote no. 2, or why. Do it anyway. — pencil, several hands

Night onewind SW 2, clear

TimeObserved
22:10Left the quay. Lamp lit.
23:41Off Heron Point. Signalled twice. Light in the watch-house, steady.
23:50No answer.

First night with the log. Was told by the skipper the hill never answers, that it's never answered, that the signal is a thing we do the way you knock on wood. Signalled anyway. Felt a fool with my arm up in the dark, and then felt better, because the light was on up there and somebody was keeping it.

— hand: R.

Night twowind W 3, clear

TimeObserved
23:38Off the Point. Signalled twice. No answer.
01:58Heron, very large, very slow, crossing low from the water toward the hill.

Not R. tonight; R. is on the morning boat. I read R.'s page and did as it said. Rule 3 says log everything, so: the heron flew straight at the watch-house as if it had business there, and went over the roof, and I lost it against the hill.

I'd like to think someone up there saw it too.

— hand: J.T.

Night threefog, thick by eleven

TimeObserved
23:44Off the Point by reckoning. Watch-house not visible.
23:45Signalled twice at where it ought to be.

Couldn't see a thing. Signalled into the fog because the log doesn't say signal if you can see it. Wrote the line neatly anyway. A night with nothing in it still gets a line.

— hand: M.

Night fourin harbour, gale

TimeObserved
—Did not sail. Read the back of the log.

With nothing to do I went through the old books in the locker. Same rules in every one, same Point, same two signals, years of no answer. Somebody, a long way back, had started writing it as a single ditto mark. Pages of them.

One entry is different. No year on it. Put in at the landing below the watch-house for water. Went up. Keeper asleep at the desk, lamp burning. Their clock was wrong, so I set it by ours — ours being eleven minutes fast, as it is and as it was. Underlined a line in their ledger that wanted underlining. Didn't sign.

So that's where our clock went. I haven't corrected ours. It seems we're keeping their time now, or they're keeping ours.

— hand: A.

Night fivewind S 1, clear, very still

TimeObserved
23:29Coming up on the Point. Lamp in hand.
23:30THE HILL SIGNALLED FIRST.
23:31Answered it. Twice.
23:32Woke the skipper.

Skipper came up in his socks and said write it down properly. Writing it down properly: at 23:30 by our clock, which is eleven minutes fast, before we had raised ours, the lamp in the watch-house on Heron Point was raised and lowered twice, deliberately. We answered.

All those years of us asking, and tonight it asked us. I said so to the skipper. He said it comes to the same thing. He said he'd been told about the signal by the man who taught him, who was told by somebody else, and none of them had seen the hill move, and none of them had stopped. Then he went below and I heard him laughing.

— hand: R. again (asked for this run)

Night sixwind S 2, clear

TimeObserved
21:50Left the quay. Early.
23:06Off the Point. Signalled twice.
23:07Answered.

We left early. Nobody gave the order. Everybody was on deck by nine with their coats on, pretending to see to things.

It answered. I've written it small this time, the ordinary size, the way you'd write the wind. I think that's the right size for it now.

— hand: J.T.

Left at the landing, in a tea tin, night seven

To the lamp,

We don't know which of you we've been signalling. The log says it's been a long time, and our hands change, and probably yours do too. That seems fine. The log remembers for us. I expect you have something like it.

Your clock is eleven minutes fast. So is ours. One of us set yours once. Leave it be; it means we're on the same time.

There was a heron, night two, at about two. If you have it, we have it too.

We'll be off the Point at half eleven, by your clock and ours. Watch the water. We'll watch the hill.

— the boat
