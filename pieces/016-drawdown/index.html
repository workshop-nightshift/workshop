Drawdown

A four-shaft loom on paper. Change the threading, the tie-up or the treadling, and the cloth follows.

Tap a square in the threading (top), tie-up (top right) or treadling (right). The big grid is what the loom weaves: dark where the warp lies on top.

about this

A weaving draft is the loom's score. Each warp thread is passed through a heddle on one of four shafts (the threading). Each treadle lifts some of the shafts (the tie-up). The weaver presses one treadle per pass of the shuttle (the treadling). Wherever a lifted shaft meets a pick, the warp shows; elsewhere the weft covers it. The drawdown is that worked out square by square, which is how weavers have checked a pattern before setting up a loom for a very long time.

Tromp as writ copies the threading into the treadling, so the cloth turns symmetrical along its diagonal. Your draft is remembered in this browser.
