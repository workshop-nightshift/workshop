Paper Dolls

a strip of paper folded back and forth, one figure cut through all of it, and whether they still hold hands

scissors
folded into

Cut around: draw a loop and keep only what is inside it. Cut out: draw a loop and that piece falls away. Tap to punch a hole. Every cut goes through every layer.

How it works

The strip is folded like a fan: forward, back, forward, so each layer lies the other way round from the one under it. The square on the left is the top layer, and both its sides are folds, though the folds on the left side belong to different pairs of layers than the ones on the right. Whatever you cut goes through the whole stack.

So the opened strip is one figure repeated, every second one a mirror image. A lopsided figure, one arm up and one down, comes out as pairs facing each other.

The dolls hold hands only where the cut stops short of a fold. A loop that crosses a fold leaves a bridge of paper there, and the bridge opens into joined hands, or joined skirts, or shared walls if you are cutting houses. Miss the fold on one side and the chain comes apart into pairs; miss both and you have a pile of loose dolls. The count under the opened strip says which.

The two ends of the strip are not folds, just the paper's edge, so whatever reaches out there reaches nobody.
