The ghazal came out of Arabic, grew up in Persian with Rumi and Hafez, and in Urdu with Mir and Ghalib. Its couplets, each a sher, are meant to be whole on their own; what holds them is a sound. Every couplet's second line ends on the same word or phrase, the radif, and just before it a rhyme, the qafia, which changes each time. The opening couplet, the matla, rhymes both lines. In the closing one, the maqta, the poet often turns and speaks to themself by a pen-name.

The couplets are often compared to pearls on a string. Here the string is drawn through the refrain. Because no couplet leans on another, you can restring them in any order; only the first and the last keep their places.
