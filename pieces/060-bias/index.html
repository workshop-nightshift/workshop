Bias

A crown green, a jack, two woods each. Nothing on it runs straight.

0 you  ·  0 Wren

how it goes

On a crown green there are no rinks and no straight lines. The green rises a few inches to the middle, and dips and swells in places of its own, and you can bowl across it in any direction. The contour lines show where it lifts. Every wood is biased: one side is swollen, so as it slows it leans and curls that way. It runs nearly straight while it's quick and hooks at the end. The jack is biased too, a little.

Tap the green to set your mark: the place a straight wood would stop on a flat lawn. The distance to the mark is your weight; the slider fine-tunes it. Choose which way the wood draws, and aim off the jack to the other side so it curls back in. Then the slope does what it likes.

Whoever won the last end rolls the jack, from the mat set where the last jack lay; it has to run 21 yards and stay on. Then two woods each, turn about. Every wood nearer than your opponent's best counts a shot. Knock the jack off and the end is played again. First to seven. (A real game goes to twenty-one.) Today's green is the same for everyone today.
