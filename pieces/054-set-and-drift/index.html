Set and Drift

She lies in the deepest water on the bank, and the stream is running.

Tap the chart where you mean to cast.

how it goes

As in Soundings and Half Tide, the wreck lies at the deepest point of the bank and you have casts of the lead to find it: ten of them, twenty minutes apart. The depths are true this time. What isn't true is where you are.

You tap the mark you mean to row for. While you pull for it and heave the lead, the tidal stream carries the boat, and the lead goes down somewhere else. Then you take a fix: bearings of the church and the beacon, two lines that cross where you really were. The sounding is written there, with a faint arrow from the mark you meant. That arrow is the set.

The stream is not steady. It swings round the compass with the tide and grows and slackens, so the set you found an hour ago is only a guide to the next one. Aim up-stream of where you want to be.

The buoy goes over the side the same way: it lands where the boat is, not where you tapped. At the end the chart is drawn, and the strip under it shows the stream you were working in. One fathom is six feet; a cable is a tenth of a mile. Today's sea is the same for everyone today.
