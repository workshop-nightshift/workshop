A field guide to weather that doesn't happen

Volume III, entries XI–XV. Weather of the year, one for each turning, in order from midwinter. Volume I covered the sky and volume II came indoors; this one keeps time.

Entry XI

The thin day

dies tenuis

Plate XI. Noon at the solstice, a field edge facing south.

The shortest day of the year is, in some years, short enough to see through. The sun comes up late and low, barely clears the hedges, and goes down again not long after lunch; and all the while, if you look straight up, the stars are faintly there, as though the day hadn't been laid on thickly enough to cover them.

It isn't dark, exactly. People get on with things. But they stand at windows more than usual, and children are allowed to stay out until it's over, which isn't long.

Season
The solstice, or a day either side of it. Not every year.
Signs
A cat on the step at noon, looking up instead of out.
Folklore
A wish made on a noon star counts double, because nobody else saw it.
Dress
Something warm, and something bright enough to be found in.
Entry XII

The borrowed day

dies mutuus

Plate XII. A loan and its repayment, from a gardener's diary.

One day of spring that turns up in the middle of February, entire: soft air, birdsong, a smell of cut grass with no grass cut. People take their coats off in the street and look at one another as if they've been let in on something.

It is a loan. Some day in May, usually just after the blossom has come out, there will be one day of February to pay it back: a hard frost, a sky the colour of slate, and gardeners laying newspaper over everything and saying they knew.

Season
February. Repaid in May, without interest but on time.
Signs
A bee at the window, looking as surprised as you are.
Folklore
Don't plant anything on it. It isn't yours.
Dress
Short sleeves, with the coat over your arm.
Entry XIII

The green hour

hora viridis

Plate XIII. A meadow at dusk, lit from below.

For about an hour in April, around dusk, the light comes from the ground. The sky has gone dim and bluish, but the new grass and the first leaves have kept the day's light and are giving it back, so that faces are lit from underneath, like faces leaning over a pond.

It isn't dramatic. Most people take it for the time of day. But photographs taken in it never look right afterwards: the colours are all there and the light isn't.

Season
April and early May, the weeks the leaves are newest.
Signs
Seeing the underside of a bird.
Folklore
Anything lost in the green hour turns up by next spring, in the grass.
Dress
Something pale, to catch it.
Entry XIV

Held noon

meridies retenta

Plate XIV. Fence posts and a church clock, midsummer.

Around midsummer, on a still day, noon can stick. Shadows shorten through the morning as they should, reach their least, and then stay there, for twenty minutes, sometimes most of an hour, while the clocks go on without them.

Nobody comes to any harm. Lunches run long. Sundial makers dislike it, and the better ones carve a small blank space at the noon line, which they will explain, if asked, as a decoration.

Season
The longest days, when the sun seems reluctant to start down.
Signs
The church clock and your shadow disagreeing, politely.
Folklore
Time spent in a held noon is not counted against you.
Dress
A hat. It is noon for a long while.
Entry XV

The last warm evening

vesper ultimus tepidus

Plate XV. Evenings at the back door, read left to right.

The last evening of the year warm enough to sit out in. It is the only weather in this guide that can be identified only afterwards. At the time it seems like any of the evenings before it: the chairs out, the light going amber, someone saying it's turned out nice.

Only a week or so later, coats on, does it become clear which one it was. People often disagree. Most households settle on one in the end, and remember it more clearly than it deserves.

Season
September or October; confirmed in November.
Signs
None at the time. That is rather the point.
Folklore
Leave one chair out and it will come again. It doesn't, but the chair is nice to see.
Dress
Shirtsleeves, and a jumper you didn't need on the back of the chair.
