Three Piles

a play in one room

Ros, the elder, has brought tape. Dan, the younger, has brought coffee.

A front room, nearly empty. The house is sold; the keys go back on Monday. Three squares of masking tape on the carpet, labelled in marker: KEEP, GIVE, BIN.

Morning

ROS is kneeling at the sideboard. DAN comes in with two paper cups and stops in the doorway.

DanYou've taped the floor.

RosI've taped the floor.

DanShe'd have hated that. The glue.

RosIt's low-tack. It says on the roll. Low-tack, it says, for delicate surfaces. Give me that.

She takes a coffee. He looks at the squares.

DanWhere's Undecided?

RosThere isn't an Undecided. That's the whole point. If there's an Undecided everything goes in Undecided and on Monday we're standing in the drive with a van full of Undecided.

DanFine. Three piles.

RosThe phone book is a bin.

DanAgreed. Is that 1998?

RosIt's 1998. She's ringed the chimney sweep.

DanShe never had the chimney swept.

RosShe ringed him, though. She was thinking about it for twenty-six years. (She lifts a wooden case off the wall.) Barometer.

DanKeep.

RosDoes it work?

DanIt says Change. It's always said Change. It said Change when I left for college and it said Change at the funeral. It's the most reliable instrument in the house.

RosKeep, then.

Dan(holding up a cake tin) Buttons.

RosGive. The charity shop on Dale Street takes buttons by the jar.

DanThere must be a thousand. Where do people get this many buttons?

RosEvery shirt you ever outgrew. She cut them off before the shirt went. You're in there about four hundred times.

DAN looks into the tin a little longer than he needs to. He puts it in GIVE.

RosThe good scissors.

DanOh, the good scissors.

RosNot for paper.

DanNot for paper, not for string, not for hair. What were they for?

RosFabric. Theoretically.

DanDid she ever cut fabric?

RosShe was keeping them good. Keep.

Dan(from a drawer) Lids. Just lids. No jars.

RosThe jars are in the garage. With no lids.

DanDo we reunite them?

RosWe do not reunite them. Bin. And the jars, later, bin.

DanTea towel. Lake Bled.

RosWho went to Lake Bled?

DanNobody went to Lake Bled. I've been thinking about it since I was nine. Nobody in this family has ever been to Slovenia.

RosSomeone brought it back for her.

DanWho? Name one person she knew who went to Slovenia.

RosGive, Dan.

Midday

The sideboard is empty. KEEP has a barometer and a pair of scissors in it. ROS opens the cupboard under the stairs and stands back.

DanOh no.

RosChristmas lights.

DanThey work. They worked last year. I did them.

RosThey're one knot. It's not lights any more, it's a single object.

DanGive me ten minutes.

RosShe gave them ten minutes every December for thirty years and so did you. Bin.

He puts them in BIN. Then he takes them out and puts them in GIVE.

DanSomeone will want a project.

Ros(she lets it go) Dad's coat.

A heavy wool coat on the last hook. Neither of them takes it down.

DanHow long has that been there?

RosSince. Nineteen years.

DanShe wore it to put the bins out.

RosShe did. In the rain.

DanSo it's been worn. It's not a shrine. It's a bin coat.

RosIt's a bin coat and it's Dad's coat.

DanKeep.

RosWhere will you put it?

DanOn a hook. I've got hooks.

He takes it down and puts it on. It is too big for him. He doesn't take it off.

Ros(the kitchen; she comes back carrying a heavy orange dish with a lid) The casserole.

DanThe casserole.

A pause. Both of them want it. Neither will say.

RosYou've got a small kitchen.

DanIt's a medium kitchen. You've got a dishwasher. That doesn't go in a dishwasher.

RosI'd wash it by hand.

DanYou've never washed anything by hand in your life.

Ros(lifting the lid) There's something in it.

A folded sheet of lined paper. She reads it, then reads it again, then hands it to him.

Dan(reading) "The orange dish. To whoever asks second."

RosThat's her writing.

Dan"Whoever asks second." What does that mean?

RosIt means she knew we'd both want it.

DanSo whoever says "can I have it" first —

Ros— loses. Yes.

DanSo neither of us says it.

RosApparently.

Silence. The dish sits between the three squares, in none of them.

DanWell, I don't want it.

RosThat's not asking. You can't win by not asking.

DanYou can't win by not asking either.

RosI'm not trying to win.

DanYou taped the floor.

The dish stays where it is. They go on around it.

RosThe dish can wait.

DanI thought there wasn't an Undecided.

RosThere isn't. It's on the floor. The floor isn't a pile.

Afternoon

Late light. Most of the room is in GIVE now; BIN is two black bags. DAN, still in the coat, pulls out the hall-table drawer and tips it onto the carpet. Keys. Thirty or forty, on rings and loose.

RosBin.

DanYou haven't looked.

RosI've looked at that drawer all my life. They're keys to nothing. That one's the old car. That one's the shed we pulled down. That one's Auntie Bea's flat and Auntie Bea has been dead since the Millennium.

DanShe kept them.

RosShe kept lids.

Dan(sorting, not listening) Gate. Bike lock. Bike lock. A tiny one, that's a diary.

RosDid she keep a diary?

DanI kept a diary. When I was eleven. Which is why that key is in this drawer and not in my room.

Ros(laughing despite herself) She took your diary key?

DanShe took my diary key. Then she gave it back and I lost it and she must have found it. She always found things. That was the trick of her. You lost a thing in this house and it turned up in this drawer a year later.

ROS kneels beside him. They sort for a while without talking.

Ros(holding one up) This one doesn't look like anything.

DanBrass. Old. Try it.

RosTry it on what?

DanOn the house. Every door. It's a house of doors. We've got till Monday.

She goes out. We hear her trying the front door, the cellar, the cupboard under the stairs. She comes back.

RosThe back door.

DanWe never used the back door.

RosIt opens the back door. It's the only key in the house that does. If we'd tried it in the last twenty years we could have used the back door.

DanWhere does it go?

RosThe garden. Where the jars are.

A pause. DAN scoops the keys back into the drawer, all of them, and puts the drawer in KEEP.

RosDan.

DanOne of them was the back door.

RosOne of forty.

DanSo it's one in forty. It's better odds than the chimney sweep.

She doesn't argue. She picks up the roll of tape, considers the empty floor, and tears a strip.

DanWhat are you doing?

Ros(writing on it) Undecided.

She makes a small fourth square around the orange dish and the brass key, and sits on the floor next to it. He sits down on the other side.

DanCan I have the dish?

Ros(at once) Can I have the dish?

They look at each other.

DanWho was first?

RosYou were first.

DanThen you win.

RosThen I'll have it. You can come for dinner. Bring the coat.

DanIt's a bin coat.

RosThen bring the bins.

On the carpet, face up in KEEP, the barometer says Change.

END

about this

A short play, read rather than staged. As you read, the things the two of them decide on move to the pile they end up in; the floor along the bottom keeps count, and tapping it opens the full lists. Scroll back and the decisions come undone. One thing waits on the carpet for a long time and isn't in any pile.
