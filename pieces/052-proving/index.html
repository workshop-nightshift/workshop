A sestina: six words, six stanzas, one night's bread.

Each line is a word, followed through the poem. Tap one.

Proving

Your six

Choose six words, then write toward them.

A sestina has no rhyme, only six words that end its lines, over and over. Each stanza takes the last stanza's words in a fixed new order: last, first, second from last, second, and in towards the middle, like folding dough. After six stanzas every word has stood in every place once, and a seventh turn would bring back the first order exactly. The three-line envoi uses all six, two to a line.

It suits subjects that come round again: a night shift, a habit, a bread that has to be made every day.
