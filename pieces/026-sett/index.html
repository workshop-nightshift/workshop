about this

Drawdown was the loom's structure: which thread lies on top where. This is the other half of a draft, the one weavers call colour-and-weave. Put the warp on in a colour order, throw the weft in a colour order, and weave something plain. The colours fall where the structure lets each thread show, and simple orders make figures the weave itself does not have.

Four dark, four light, both ways, in a two-and-two twill: houndstooth. The same order in plain weave: little stepped checks. One dark, one light in plain weave with the weft in step: fine crosswise lines. Shift the weft by one thread and the lines stand up instead. Swap the phase every few threads and you get log cabin, blocks of lines turning. Try interlacing only to see how little of the pattern is in the weave.

A sett is the colour order of a check or a tartan, written as thread counts, and also how closely the threads are set. Your cloth is remembered in this browser.
