the positions, as you see them

    about this

    Flag semaphore: each letter is a pair of arm positions, out of eight around the body, held long enough to be read through a glass. The letters go round in circles. A to G keep one arm down and walk the other round; H to N keep one arm low and out and walk the other; and so on, each circle a little shorter, until Z. J came late and was fitted in where there was room, which is why it sits outside the order.

    Figures are sent with the same positions: a numeral sign first, then A for one through I for nine, and K for nought. J, held after them, means letters follow. Between words the arms drop and cross in front. A doubled letter is sent twice with a dip between, so the reader can count it.

    Everything here is drawn as you, the reader, see it: the signaller's right arm is on your left. In the read tab the word comes from a short list of things worth saying across water. Your signal stays in the address, so it can be passed on.
