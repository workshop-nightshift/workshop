Your bell
Pick a bell and press start. Sound will play.

In a tower each bell has one ringer, and the band's whole art is to strike evenly: six blows spaced like a ticking clock, a little gap, six more. Here five of the ringers are perfect and never tire. The sixth rope is yours. Tap the button (or the space bar) when your bell's turn comes in each row.

The blue line is your bell's path through the rows, the way ringers learn a method: not as a list of numbers but as a shape to follow. Plain hunt takes twelve changes and every bell's line is the same zigzag. In plain bob the treble (red) keeps hunting while the others dodge at each lead end, and it comes round after sixty.

Each tap is drawn where it fell: a dot dead on your number is perfect, a dot to the left was early (a clip), to the right was late (a drag). When the bells stand you get the whole touch as a striking chart. Two rounds are rung for you first, to listen. Headphones help; so does the slowest pace.
