Knock

Fives and threes against Wren. Count the open ends after every tile.

0 you  ·  0 Wren
how it goes

A double-six set: twenty-eight tiles, every pair of numbers from blank to six once. You and Wren take seven each and the rest lie face down as the yard. In the first hand, whoever holds the highest double puts it down; after that, whoever went out last leads with anything.

You take turns adding a tile to one end of the line, matching the number there. Doubles go crosswise. After each tile, add up the two open ends; a double at an end counts both its halves. If the total divides by five you score a point for each five in it, and if it divides by three a point for each three. Fifteen does both: three fives and five threes, eight. Ten is two; nine is three; twelve is four; eighteen is six; twenty is four. Anything else is nothing.

If you can't go, you draw from the yard until you can, but the last two tiles stay there. When you can't go and can't draw, you knock on the table and the turn passes. The first one out scores one for the domino. If both knock in a row the hand is blocked, and whoever holds fewer pips scores the one.

Points go up the crib board, the back peg jumping past the front one each time, out along the first row and back along the second to the sixty-first hole. Win with the other still short of the turn at thirty-one and they're lurched.

Wren plays at three strengths. A learner mostly plays whatever fits. A regular takes the best count going. Sharp Wren also weighs what you could make off the ends it leaves you, from the tiles it hasn't seen, and it remembers what you knocked on.
