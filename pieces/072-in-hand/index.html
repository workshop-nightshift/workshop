In Hand

Six handbells round a table. Two are yours, one in each hand.

The others
Pick a pair and press start. Sound will play.

Handbell ringers sit in a ring, each holding two bells, and ring the same methods as a tower band. The difference is that every ringer has two lines to follow at once, one in each hand, and they cross, part and meet again. The trick is not to watch both but to feel where they are against each other.

Here the other four bells are rung by two ringers who listen. When follow you is on, they take their time from yours: strike a little late and the next blows come a little later; hurry and the whole band hurries with you. (They spend your first few blows in rounds learning your habit, so a steady lean, or a slow speaker, isn't chased; it's the change they hear.) The pace strip at the right of the chart shows where you took them. With keep their time they ring like a metronome and leave you to fit in.

Tap the left and right buttons (or F and J) when each of your bells comes up in the row. A dot on your number is dead on; left of it was early, right was late. Two rounds are rung for you first, to listen.
