how it's made

Each bird looks at the six or seven birds nearest to it, whatever the distance, and steers three ways at once: away from any that are too close, along the way they are going, and towards where they are. That is the whole of the flock's mind. Field studies of real starlings found roughly that rule: a bird keeps track of a handful of neighbours, not of everyone inside some radius, which is why a flock can thin and thicken and still hold together.

The dark bands are turns. A bird that sees the falcon rolls away, and a bird that sees a neighbour roll rolls too, a moment later. Rolling shows the wing broadside, so the turn reads as a shadow running through the flock, faster than any bird in it is flying. Nothing much moves; the wave is all handed on.

Then the light goes, and the flock starts to pour down out of itself into the reeds, the lowest first, until the sky is empty and the reedbed is full of talk.
