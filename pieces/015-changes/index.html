In a ringing tower the bells can't play tunes: each one swings full circle and takes a couple of seconds to come round, so all a band can do is change the order in which they strike, a little at a time. A bell may only move one place per row. Rounds is 1 2 3 4 5 6, high to low. From there the rows are rearranged by rule, and the aim is never to ring the same row twice before the bells come back to rounds.

Plain hunt is the simplest rule: everyone swaps, then everyone but the ends swaps, and it comes round after twelve rows. Plain bob lets the treble hunt like that while the others are nudged at every lead end, and comes round after sixty. A bob or a single changes what happens at the next lead end, and sends the bells somewhere they haven't been. There are 720 rows on six bells. Call well and you can ring every one of them without repeating. Call badly and a row comes up twice, and it's marked red. Or let the conductor do it: one way through all 720, found by a small search the night this was made. (Keys: B and S call.)

The red line is the treble. The blue line is the bell you follow. There is a short gap every other row, the way a tower rings, hand then back.
