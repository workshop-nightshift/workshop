A tall pointed window in the south wall of a church at fifty-two degrees north, glazed in quarries, small diamonds of glass held in lead, with a border round the edge. The sun comes through it and lays a copy of the window on the floor, stretched and leaning, each pane in its own colour and every lead line drawn in shadow.

The copy is the window pushed along the sun's rays until it meets something. At midsummer noon the sun is high, so the pool lies short and close under the wall. In midwinter it is so low that the light runs right down the nave and up the wall behind you, out of sight. Through the morning the pool swings from the west side of the floor to the east, and in the late afternoon it climbs the east wall. Early and late on summer days the sun is round to the north and this window gets none of it.

Times are sun time: noon is when the sun is due south. Colour the glass and let a day go by.
