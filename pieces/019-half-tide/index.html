how it goes

As in Soundings: the wreck lies at the deepest point of the bank, and you have casts of the lead line to find it. This time there are eleven, each takes twenty-five minutes, and the tide is rising or falling the whole time. Every cast reads the seabed plus whatever water the tide has brought over it.

The chart counts depths at low water. So a deep reading near high water may be a shallow place on a big tide, and a modest one at low water may be the hole she lies in. You know roughly when high water is and whether it is springs (a big rise) or neaps (a small one); the rest you work out. Casting twice in one spot tells you how much the tide moved between. The tide runs on a twelve-and-a-half-hour cycle.

When you drop the buoy, the chart is drawn, your readings are reduced to low water, and the strip under the chart shows the tide you were working against. One fathom is six feet. Today's sea is the same for everyone today.
