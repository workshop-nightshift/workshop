An island, and the lines a surveyor would draw on it.

Press with your finger or the mouse to raise land out of the sea; hold still and it keeps rising. Switch to lower to cut a valley, smooth to weather it. Drag the red A and B to cut a section through the hills.

Reading the lines

Each thin line joins ground at the same height, every ten metres; every fifth line is heavier and carries its height. The numbers stand the way a surveyor stands them: their tops face uphill.

Where the lines crowd together the ground is steep; where they open out it is gentle. Cut your section across a crowded patch and watch the profile climb.

Lines bent into a V that points uphill are a valley: water would run down its middle. A V pointing downhill is a spur. Two hills with a low saddle between them meet at a col, where the lines turn their backs on each other.

Below the sea the lines go blue and count down instead. The thick dark line is the one every map has first: height nought, the coast.

The island is two kilometres across. Your changes stay on this device.
