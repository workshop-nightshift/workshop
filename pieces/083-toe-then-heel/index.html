Terza rima is Dante’s: the whole of the Commedia is written in it, three-line stanzas rhyming aba bcb cdc, each middle line handing its rhyme on to the stanza after. Nothing ever quite closes until the end, where one line by itself catches the last loose rhyme.

Italian has rhymes to spare and English doesn’t, so the form has always been a stretch here. Shelley built the “Ode to the West Wind” out of it, and Frost’s “Acquainted with the Night” is a short one. The chain on the right of the poem draws each rhyme sound from the line that puts it out to the two that pick it up; the step you are on shows the foot behind in blue and the foot ahead in red.
