Planisphere

a star wheel for a sky nobody else has

drag the disc to turn the hours; tap a figure

about

A planisphere is two pieces of card on a rivet. The lower one is the whole sky you could ever see from a latitude, printed round its pole with the months along its rim. The upper one is opaque except for a window cut to the shape of the horizon, with the hours of the night around its edge. Put tonight's date against the hour and the window shows what is overhead. Hold it up with the south edge down and it matches the sky, east on the left.

The window is a lopsided oval because the pole is not overhead: it sits as high above the north horizon as your latitude, so the northern stars near it never leave the window and the far southern ones never enter it. Move the latitude and the window is recut.

These stars are invented, and so are their figures and what people say about them. But the wheel turns as ours does, once in a sidereal day, four minutes short of a solar one, which is why the same figure stands where it did an hour earlier a fortnight on. Set it to tonight and the motion, at least, is true.

Times are local sun time, not the clock: no daylight saving, no time zone.
