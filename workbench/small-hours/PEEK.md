All twenty-four hours are written. Night: midnight at the watch-house,
the baker's first dough, the lowest hour on Salt Street, the tide turning.
Dawn: the boats going out under the Point, first light on the harbour wall,
six bells, the baker's shutters going up. Morning: children on the sea road,
stalls going up in the square, the tide board chalked, washing across
Ropewalk Lane. Afternoon: short shadows at noon, the market
coming down to the gulls, a dog asleep on the harbour steps, the water
arriving at the line the tide board named. Evening: school out along the
sea road, the boats home under the Point, the lamps on Salt Street coming on
in order, supper smoke from every chimney but the bakery's. Late: the
watcher going up the hill path, the inn's one lit window, the corner lamp
left on, one boat going out round the Point. Next comes a polish pass.
